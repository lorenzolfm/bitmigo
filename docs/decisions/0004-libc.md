# 0004 — `libc`, for signals, in the node binary only

**Status:** accepted, 2026-09-09.

## Decision

`libc` is the fourth third-party crate, pinned `=0.2.189`, and a dependency of
`crates/bitmigo` alone. `crates/consensus` does not depend on it; the purity rules of
[decision 0001](0001-primitive-crates.md) are otherwise unchanged, and no further crate
arrives without a record of its own.

It is used for one thing: installing handlers for `SIGINT` and `SIGTERM`, the byte a handler
writes to the shutdown self-pipe, the `poll` that lets the listener wait on that pipe and on
its listening socket at once, and the immediate `_exit` a second signal performs. That is the
whole of it, and it is confined to one module, `runtime::signal`, which is also the only
place in the workspace allowed to write `unsafe`.

## Why

`std` supplies everything else the node needs from the operating system: read and write
timeouts on sockets, `shutdown`, positioned reads through `FileExt::read_at`, `fsync`,
`UnixListener`, explicit thread stack sizes, `available_parallelism`. Signal handling is the
one gap, and it is not a small one for this program:

- **A clean stop must exist.** Without signals, `SIGINT` and `SIGTERM` kill the process where
  it stands, so every Ctrl-C and every `systemctl stop` becomes a crash-recovery restart that
  replays each block since the last flush. That is worst during exactly the multi-day initial
  sync where it costs most.
- **Crash consistency is a backstop, not a shutdown plan.** The storage engine has to survive
  a power cut, but relying on that for the ordinary case would mean never testing the ordinary
  case.
- **The cost is close to zero.** `libc` is maintained by the rust-lang organisation, has no
  transitive dependencies, and is already linked into every Rust program through `std`. Adding
  it changes the dependency graph by one node that was in the process anyway.

## Alternatives considered

- **No signals at all**, with a `bitmigo stop` command over the control socket as the
  documented clean stop and systemd's `ExecStop=` covering the service case. Declined: it
  leaves Ctrl-C — the way a person actually stops a program they are watching — as the
  destructive path, and it makes an operator's habit into a data-loss event.
- **Writing the `sigaction` binding by hand.** It is a handful of `extern "C"` declarations
  and a struct whose layout differs per platform and per libc. Hand-maintaining that struct
  is the sort of quiet portability bug this project exists to avoid, and it would be `unsafe`
  either way — the same risk with none of the review `libc` gets.

## Consequences

- The workspace lint is `unsafe_code = "deny"` rather than `forbid`, so that one module can
  lift it with an `#[allow]` that names its reason. Every other crate root states
  `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, which cannot be lifted at all, so the exception stays visible and
  countable: one module, in one crate.
- What a handler may do is fixed by what is async-signal-safe: an atomic store, a `write` to
  the self-pipe, and `_exit`. Nothing else belongs there, and the module's comments say so.
- The pin moves the way the other three do — a bump commit that names what changed.
